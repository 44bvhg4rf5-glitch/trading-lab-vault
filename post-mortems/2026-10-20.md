# Post-mortem 2026-10-20

Today's paper trading session closed with a modest gain of 0.29%, bringing equity to $104,070.25, but this masked significant underlying issues. The choppy regime contributed to poor shadow performance, with a day R of -442 across 469 resolved shadows, indicating that simulated trades struggled. Only one thesis was executed out of seven, while eight were gate-blocked and four expired, suggesting overly restrictive filters or weak thesis generation in this environment. Technical errors in position adoption for several symbols (e.g., AVGO, BTCUSD) due to unrecognizable structures were left unresolved, pointing to process vulnerabilities. While the small real gain might be attributed to lucky execution, the bulk of the activity was process-driven, with gate-blocking and data errors amplifying the negative shadow outcomes. Overall, the day highlights a disconnect between actual execution and simulated risk, underscoring the need for better alignment between thesis generation and market conditions.

## Lessons filed

- **process**: Multiple position adoption errors from unrecognizable structures reveal weaknesses in data validation that could lead to unmonitored risks if not addressed.
- **execution**: With only one execution out of seven theses, the gate-blocking mechanism may be too stringent for choppy regimes, potentially missing valid opportunities.
- **risk**: The substantial shadow loss of -442 R emphasizes that simulated trading can accumulate risks even without real capital at stake, requiring closer monitoring of shadow metrics.

## Hypotheses to test

- If the gate criteria are adjusted to be more adaptive to regime changes, execution frequency might increase without proportionally raising drawdown risks.
- Improving the position adoption process by adding fallback validation rules could reduce errors and enhance overall system reliability.
