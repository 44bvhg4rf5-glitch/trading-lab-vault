## The 5 best, ranked — #1 first

### 1. NVDA — Nvidia — BUY NOW
$195–197, forward P/E ~23.2x — 23% below the semiconductor sector median (~30x) *and* 44% below Nvidia's own 10-year median (52.8x). That double discount, on the single largest AI bellwether in the market, after a "summer swoon" pullback from resistance near $212, is the cleanest mispricing in this entire universe. Last quarter's revenue was +85% YoY ($81.6B), Data Center +92% ($75.2B). Consensus is Strong Buy (~57% Strong Buy of 61–68 analysts), average target ~$303 — roughly 54% above the current price. Real risk: China data-center revenue is guided at zero and insider-selling headlines linger, but neither shows up in the multiple, which is the point. No earnings landmine between now and the next few weeks.

### 2. AVGO — Broadcom — BUY NOW
~$382–400, forward P/E ~20x — 41% below the semiconductor sector median, cheaper than even NVDA on a forward basis, while guiding AI semiconductor revenue to grow >200% YoY next quarter, the fastest guided growth rate of any name in this report. Trailing P/E looks rich (~59–67x) only because the market is pricing a sharp near-term earnings inflection, not because the stock is expensive going forward. Up only ~4% YTD in 2026 versus AMD's ~142% — the clear laggard of the AI-chip trade despite arguably the best forward setup. Target ~$520–525 (~37% upside). Strong Buy consensus (~85% buy-rated). A new "Jalapeño" custom AI chip deal with OpenAI (late June) and the Apple supply agreement extended through 2031 both underline demand breadth beyond Nvidia's own ecosystem.

### 3. DIS — Disney — BUY
~$97–99, forward P/E ~15.3x — 41% below the broader market average. Disney+/Hulu streaming operating income was +88% last quarter to $582M, the first quarter streaming margin broke into double digits (10.6%), and management is guiding to 10%+ for the full fiscal year — a real profitability inflection, not a promise. The analyst target (~$127–129) implies the single largest upside gap (28–31%) of any name in this report. The drag is ESPN (operating income −5%, ad sales soft on fewer NBA games), but that's a known, bounded problem next to a streaming business that just proved it can make real money. No earnings due for several weeks.

### 4. HD — Home Depot — BUY
~$310–313, P/E ~22–25x — sitting right at Home Depot's own 10-year average (~22.5–23.5x), so this isn't a statistically "cheap" call, it's a clean one: Q1 beat on both lines (EPS $3.43 vs $3.41 est., revenue $41.8B vs $41.51B est.), guidance reaffirmed, comps positive, Strong Buy consensus (8.2/10), target ~$370–374 (11–20% upside). Management calls tariff cost pass-through "modest" and "not broad based." This is the most boring name in the top 5 on purpose — no binary event in the next few weeks, no valuation controversy, just a well-run business trading at its own historical multiple with double-digit analyst upside.

### 5. ETH — Ethereum — BUY / ADD
~$1,857–1,881, up +24.3% over the trailing month — the strongest momentum of anything in this report, equity or crypto. Unlike most crypto catalysts, this one already happened rather than being promised: the December 2025 Fusaka upgrade cut node bandwidth/storage needs ~85% and reduced Layer-2 transaction fees 60–90%, a delivered scaling result, not a roadmap slide. Ethereum ETFs have pulled in $29.22B in net inflows, AUM growing from $10.3B to $28.6B in roughly a year. Genuine uncertainty here: a secondary source claims ETH is "70% below ATH" but the precise all-time-high figure couldn't be confirmed independently, so treat that specific comparison as unverified. This is also one of only two names (with BTC) the engine already trades live, 24/7, on its own crypto risk budget — no new integration needed to act on this one.

## On deck — strong cases, wrong week

Four equities plus BTC would plausibly be top-5 material on valuation alone, but each walks into a binary event within about 48 hours of this note — exactly the situation Trading Lab's own earnings guard and macro guard exist to sit out. "Right away" doesn't apply to any of these yet:

- **MSFT** (~$389–397) — near a 1-year low, cheapest forward P/E (~21x) since 2023, Azure still compounding near 40% growth, target ~$557–592 (40%+ upside) — but reports July 29. The de-rating looks like a capex-optics problem, not a demand problem; worth revisiting the moment the print clears.
- **META** (~$594–602) — trading ~26% below its own historical multiple, target 36–38% above spot, already down ~25% from its 52-week high — but also reports July 29, an event options are pricing an ~8% move around.
- **AMZN** (~$232, stale) — multiple compressed to roughly half its trailing 5-year average even as AWS growth just reaccelerated to a 15-quarter high — but reports July 30, and the most recent confirmable price point was already several days old at research time.
- **PG** (~$147–153) — a reasonable 21.8x multiple and double-digit analyst upside, but earnings land the very next day (July 29) and the restructuring (7,000 role cuts) is unproven. The researcher's own word for this one was "coin-flip" — disqualifying for a right-away call.
- **BTC** (~$63,200–63,700) — down ~50% from its October 2025 all-time high, a Fear-Greed reading of 26, a genuine institutional ETF-inflow reversal in mid-July: a real contrarian case. But the Fed's own meeting concludes July 28–29 with a hawkish tilt already weighing on risk assets — precisely the situation the system's macro guard is built for. Wait for the decision, then decide.
- **CRM** (~$165) — the cheapest valuation Salesforce has carried in years (51% discount to the tech sector average) — but three sell-side downgrades in three weeks (Morgan Stanley, KeyBanc, Bernstein) on real growth deceleration, not just sentiment. A contrarian watchlist idea, not a conviction call.

## The short / avoid list — informational only, the system cannot act on this

Trading Lab is long-only end to end; nothing below can become a live position here. Flagging them anyway because a director's brief isn't complete without the other side of the book:

- **TSLA** (~$306) — trailing P/E ~283–312x, reportedly ~15x the auto industry's median, on negative free cash flow (~−$3.25B last quarter against $6.7B of AI/robotaxi/Optimus capex). Priced almost entirely on unproven robotaxi optionality Musk himself says won't show material revenue before 2027.
- **AAPL** (~$333–336) — trailing P/E ~40.3x, the richest multiple of any mega-cap tech peer, ~50% above its own 10-year median — and unusually, the sell-side's own average target (~$319–323) now sits *below* the current price, one week before earnings.
- **AMD** (~$539–544) — forward P/E ~66x, still roughly 2x the semiconductor sector median even after a +140–160% YTD run; the average target (~$542–575) has essentially caught up to the price, leaving little embedded margin of safety.
- **CVX** (~$185–190) — trailing P/E ~30–31.5x, 38% above its own 10-year average and 86% above the energy sector average — the richest relative multiple of any name in this report, on a quarter with profit down 37% YoY.
- **WMT** (~$109–113) — trailing P/E ~39.5–40x, 25–31% above its own history and 72% above its sector average, walking into rising tariff-driven cost pressure and a Mexico-division profit miss.

## Fairly valued — priced for perfection, best left alone

- **GOOGL** (~$326–328) — the headline ~16x trailing P/E is a data trap: ~$6–7 of last quarter's EPS came from non-cash mark-to-market gains on Alphabet's Anthropic/SpaceX stakes. Strip that out and the real forward multiple (~24–26x) is in line with peers. Solid core business (Cloud backlog >$462B), but a $195–205B capex hike and two live EU fines (a ~€4.1B Android appeal loss, a fresh ~€890M DMA fine) are real costs, not noise.
- **JPM** (~$345–356, all-time high) — an all-time-best quarter (EPS $7.70 vs $5.85 est.), but analyst targets already sit roughly where the stock trades. Leaning slightly overvalued: little room for the current run of dealmaking strength to disappoint.
- **BAC** (~$62, all-time high) — beat estimates, but the average target (~$64–65) is barely above the current price, and a proposed 10% credit-card interest-rate cap is a real, current regulatory overhang.
- **XOM** (~$155–157) — trailing P/E near a 5-year high, driven by a geopolitical (Strait of Hormuz) oil-price spike that has reversed once already this year; the "cheap" forward multiple only holds if the rally does.
- **UNH** (~$417–426) — a genuine operational turnaround (medical cost ratio at a 2-year low, guidance raised) has already re-rated the stock 44% above its own historical multiple, with a DOJ investigation into Medicare Advantage billing still unresolved.
- **JNJ** (~$260–269, all-time high) — a real de-risking event (a ~$5.5B talc-litigation settlement) plus an earnings beat, but sell-side targets cluster at/below the current price.
- **KO** (~$81, all-time high) — the best quarter in the group (unit case volume +5%, the largest jump in 17 years, largely World Cup-driven), but that tailwind won't repeat and the stock already trades above its sector-average multiple.
- **COST** (~$920–935) — a 46x trailing multiple is normal for Costco's membership-model quality, but there's little cushion left after a recent comparable-sales wobble and a 14% pullback from all-time highs.
- **PEP** (~$137–140) — unlike KO, this one reads as genuine demand softness (management's own words: "consumer budgets tightened"), not just an unloved multiple. The Hold-heavy analyst base agrees.
- **NFLX** (~$70–71, post 10-for-1 split) — the multiple has compressed to a rare discount, but management's own decision to disclose fewer forward engagement metrics right before the sell-off is a real visibility problem the cheap headline multiple doesn't resolve.

## Methodology

Built from two independent rounds of live web research (26 assets total: AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, AMZN, GOOGL, META, TSLA, AMD, AVGO, NFLX, JPM, BAC, XOM, CVX, UNH, JNJ, PG, KO, WMT, HD, DIS, CRM, COST, PEP, BTC, ETH), each covering price/trend, valuation vs. own history and sector, analyst sentiment and price target, the live catalyst, and an explicit verdict. Sources were financial data aggregators (GuruFocus, StockAnalysis.com, MarketBeat/S&P Global-style polls, macrotrends) and financial news (CNBC, Bloomberg, Reuters wires, Motley Fool, TradingKey, 24/7 Wall St.), all from roughly July 1–28, 2026. Ranked by: strength of the valuation/catalyst combination, analyst-implied upside, and — deliberately — absence of a binary event (earnings, Fed decision) in the immediate future, since a "right away" call that walks straight into a coin-flip isn't really a right-away call.
